# 2-minute demo video — shot list

Shoot on a phone, horizontal, one take per shot. Total 8 shots. You can film this in twenty
minutes and cut it in ten.

**Audio:** record your voiceover separately on the phone's voice memo app, in a quiet room, then
lay it over the footage. Do not try to narrate while filming, the room noise will ruin it.

---

## Shot 1 — 0:00–0:12 · Title card
Slide 1 of the PDF, full screen, screen-recorded or exported PNG from `slides/s01.png`.

> "Four days ago, six hundred metres of glacier fell twelve hundred metres into a valley in Nepal.
> Six hundred and sixty-nine people are dead. Two thousand nine hundred are still missing."

## Shot 2 — 0:12–0:24 · The problem
`slides/s04.png` then `slides/s05.png`.

> "The warning was minutes. The rescue takes hours. And people trapped in rubble don't mainly die
> of injury, they die of shock and of giving up."

## Shot 3 — 0:24–0:38 · The job that doesn't scale
`slides/s05.png` held, or the rescuer photo.

> "So before anyone lifts a slab, a rescuer lies face down in the silt with one arm through a gap,
> talking into the dark, for six hours. It's the most important job on the site. It's also the one
> job that cannot scale, cannot fit into a tunnel, and has to stop when the lake upstream moves."

## Shot 4 — 0:38–0:48 · The reveal
`slides/s06.png`.

> "So we built the hand. Saathi. Companion, in Nepali."

## Shot 5 — 0:48–1:20 · THE DEMO. This is the shot that matters.
**Live footage of the arm.** Frame it tight, from the side, so both the gripper and the hand are
in frame. Get a friend's hand in the shot, not your own, so you're free to trigger it.

Sequence in one unbroken take, no cuts:
1. Hand enters frame, opens flat.
2. Arm closes on it. Hold for three full seconds so it reads clearly.
3. The Nepali line plays — leave the real audio in for this bit, do not talk over it.
4. Hand gently pulls away, arm stays where it is.

> "It closes on force, not position. It's told how hard it may squeeze, not where to stop, so it
> physically cannot grip harder than a handshake. Then it talks to them in Nepali."

**If the arm is not working:** film the gripper closing on the hand by hand-teleoperating it with
the leader arm, and say "teleoperated here" in the voiceover. An honest teleop shot beats a
fake autonomous one, and judges can always tell.

## Shot 6 — 1:20–1:35 · Why offline
`slides/s09.png`.

> "There's no network in that valley. Forty-two kilometres of road, every bridge, and the towers
> went with the valley floor. So the speech runs on the robot itself, on a Nepali model built by a
> lab in Kathmandu."

## Shot 7 — 1:35–1:50 · Honest scope
`slides/s13.png`.

> "The hand is real, and you just watched it. The body is a render. Current arm hardware is far too
> heavy for the deployed version and we're not pretending otherwise."

## Shot 8 — 1:50–2:00 · Close
`slides/s14.png`, hold on it in silence for the last two seconds.

> "It doesn't dig. It doesn't lift. It stays."

## Editing notes

- Cut on the beat of the voiceover, not on the visuals.
- No music, or something very quiet and sparse. Music over a disaster reads badly.
- Do not add sound effects to the robot. The real servo noise is better.
- Burn no text captions except the title and the closing line. The voiceover carries it.
- Export 1080p, under 100 MB so the upload doesn't fail at the deadline.

## The one rule

Shot 5 is sixty percent of your score. If you are short on time, cut shots 2, 3 and 7 down to
four seconds each and give the extra time to the demo. Nobody has ever lost a hackathon for
under-explaining the problem, and plenty have lost for a demo nobody could see.
